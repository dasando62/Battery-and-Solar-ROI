Guide: Scenario - Modelling EV Charging

This guide explains how to use the "Electric Vehicle (EV) Charging" feature. This allows you to simulate the financial impact of adding an EV to your household, showing how a solar and battery system can help offset the new charging costs.

Prerequisite: Before starting, you should have already set up your main household data (either via CSV/NEM12 upload or Manual Mode bill entry). The EV charging load will be added on top of this existing household load.

Step 1: Enable & Configure the EV

Go to the collapsible section titled "Electric Vehicle (EV) Charging" (located just below Section 1: Data Input).

  • Enable EV Charging Simulation: Check this box to turn the feature on. This will add the simulated charging load to both your "Baseline" cost and your "New System" cost calculations.
  • Average Daily Driving (km): Enter the average number of kilometres you drive (or plan to drive) each day.
  • EV Efficiency (kWh / 100km): Enter your EV's energy efficiency.

    You can find this on your car's dashboard or by a quick search. Typical values are 15-18 kWh/100km for efficient models (like a Tesla Model 3) or 20-25 kWh/100km for larger electric SUVs.

  • Minimum Solar Battery % for EV Charging: This is a crucial setting to protect your home's battery. It sets a "reserve" level. The EV will not be allowed to charge from the home battery (using a "From Battery" rule) if the battery's charge is below this percentage. This ensures you save enough power for your household's evening peak usage.

Step 2: Set Up Charging Rules (in Section 5)

This is the most important part, as it defines how and >when your EV charges. The analyser lets you set different charging strategies for each provider, which is configured in Section 5: Providers & Tariffs.

Go to a provider's details and find the "EV Charging Rules" section.

The analyser processes these rules in Priority Order (top to bottom). It tries to fulfill the day's total EV charging need (calculated from Step 1) using the first rule. Any remaining need is passed to the second rule, and so on.

For each rule, you set the Source and Time:

  • Source: "From Excess Solar"
    This is "smart charging". The EV will only charge during the specified hours (e.g., "9am-4pm") and only using solar power that would have otherwise been exported to the grid. This is the cheapest way to charge.
  • Source: "From Battery"
    This allows the EV to charge from your home battery during the specified hours. This is useful if the EV arrives home *after* the sun has gone down but you still want to use stored solar. It respects the "Minimum Solar Battery %" you set in Step 1.
  • Source: "From Grid"
    This is "dumb charging" from the grid. The EV will pull its required power from the grid during the specified hours. This is your "last resort" rule to ensure the car is charged by morning.

Recommended Strategy Example:

  • Rule 1: Source: From Excess Solar, Hours: 9am-4pm (Charges from free solar first)
  • Rule 2: Source: From Grid, Hours: 11pm-6am (Tops up from cheap off-peak grid power)

This strategy ensures the EV prioritises solar and only uses the grid during the cheapest off-peak times, maximizing your savings.

Ensure the rest of your analyser setup is complete. The EV simulation will interact with all other parts of your configuration (your battery size, inverter limits, etc.).

  • Section 3: New System

Step 4: Run Analysis & Interpret Results

Click the Run ROI Analysis button. When you enable EV charging, the simulated EV load is added to both your "Baseline Cost" and your "New System Cost".

  • Your Baseline Cost will be higher (reflecting charging the EV from the grid).
  • Your New System Cost will also be higher (reflecting the EV charging), but your Total Savings should be much larger, as the solar/battery system is now offsetting these new, expensive charging costs in addition to your normal household load.

This allows you to accurately model the additional value a solar & battery system provides specifically for an EV owner.
